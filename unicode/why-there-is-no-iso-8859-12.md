# Why there is no ISO-8859-12

The ISO 8859 character sets go `-1` to `-11`, then `-13` to `-16`. Part 12 doesn't exist.

* it was first proposed for the **Celtic languages**
* the number was then given to **Latin/Devanagari**, and the Celtic proposal moved on: it became **ISO-8859-14 (Latin-8, Celtic)** in 1998, with the dotted consonants of Irish (`ḃ ċ ḋ ḟ ġ ṁ ṗ ṡ ṫ`) and Welsh `ŵ ŷ`
* Devanagari then stalled at the **12th meeting** of the working group (ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 2/WG 3), in **Iraklion, Crete, 4–7 July 1997**. Not voted down, just parked. Resolution M 12.08: "WG 3 resolves to suspend any activities on this subject until general agreement on combining characters is obtained and until the further contributions are received."
* Thai (8859-11) got the same treatment at the same meeting for the same reason (combining characters), but was published anyway in 2001. Devanagari never came back: India already had its own standard (ISCII), and Unicode covers it today.
* the working group disbanded in June 2004, so 12 stays empty forever

Was there a party in Greece? The minutes only go as far as resolution M 12.21, thanking the hosts for "beautiful Iraklion-Crete", the only resolution passed "By ACCLAMATION".

The joke version, quoted on the Unicode mailing list in 1999 (Markus Kuhn's satire): a US delegate wanted to skip the unlucky part 13, a British delegate objected, and as a compromise they removed 13 − 1 = 12.
